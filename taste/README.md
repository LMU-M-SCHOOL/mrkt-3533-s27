# Teardowns

## What a teardown is

The machines can crank out endless ideas. Your job is to prompt and select the right ones. That is the skill this course builds, and the weekly teardown is how you practice it before your own work is on the line.

A teardown takes one recent piece of marketing work apart the way an Effie judge would. You decide whether it is on brand, then you score it in the four sections judges score, and you say what you would change. You will do this every week on other people's work, so that by the time you judge your own agent's output you have a trained eye and the words to explain what you see.

## The gate

Before scoring, one question: is it on brand? Pass or fail, with one reason. For agent output and your own work, tie the reason to the client's style guide or voice. For a public campaign, tie it to the brand's visible identity and name two earlier pieces from the same brand as evidence. The gate is never not applicable. Work that fails the gate is not scored.

## The four sections

Insight. Is there a true human or category truth driving this? What led to the idea?

Idea. Would a person repeat it? Is it a new way in?

Execution. Is it crafted? Does it come to life across channels, and does the craft serve the idea?

Results. Did it work, or how would we know? For fresh work, judge the measurement plan: specific objectives, KPIs that match them, and a credible evidence standard.

## The scale

Score each section from 1 to 5. A 3 is competent professional work. A 5 is work you would show a hiring manager as the standard. A 1 is work that fails the section outright. Scores are your honest rating of the work, not your grade.

## How teardowns are graded

Your grade is for the quality of your reasoning, not for how close your scores land to anyone else's. Each of the four sections earns up to 6 points: a clear claim (0 to 2), evidence drawn from the work itself (0 to 2), and a so-what that says why it matters for the brand (0 to 2). The gate reason earns up to 2 more. That is 26 points per teardown. The weekly teardowns together are about a fifth of the course grade.

Evidence means something you can point to in the piece: a line of copy, a casting choice, a channel, a number. A teardown you could write from the press release scores low on evidence.

## The seen-log

For every piece you tear down, you should see 15 to 25 more. Log each one in your seen-log file as one line: date, source, title, URL, separated by a vertical bar. The log is checked for volume, not graded. It exists so the exposure happens, because taste moves with what you look at, not with what you write about.

## How to submit

1. Direct your office agent (Claude Cowork or ChatGPT Work) to research the piece and fill in TEMPLATE.md. Give it the source URL and the brand's two earlier pieces.
2. Read what it wrote. Fix anything you would not say yourself. The agent drafts, you decide.
3. Save the file as `.md` into the shared inbox folder. Name it however you like. The instructor's script validates it, files it under your initials, and opens the pull request.

Your archive entry carries your initials only. After class, if your pick was discussed, add the class scores to your entry.
